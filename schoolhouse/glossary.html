Glossary

Three tiers: the field’s domain vocabulary, the house’s named strategies, and the house’s own coined terms. Entries marked needs definition are scaffolded — in use throughout the house but not yet formally defined here.

Tier 1

Core Concept Terms

Domain vocabulary the research uses. The house treats these as defined atoms; most link to concept pages not yet built — this glossary is where they live until those pages exist.

Attention

Finite and easily captured by whatever is most salient. Protected by clean transitions and low extraneous load.

→ Concept Map (Science of Learning layer)

Behavior & Redirections

What redirection looks like when it works — naming the deed, not the doer; the difference between correction and punishment.

→ Concept Map (5.1) · 5.1 Behavior & Engagement

Belonging

The belief that one has a place in this room and in this community — precondition for engagement, risk-taking, and the willingness to try again.

→ Concept Map (5.3) · 5.3 Culture & Belonging

Cognitive Load

The mind’s workspace is limited; extraneous load crowds out the germane load that is the actual learning. Classroom management, viewed through this lens, is largely extraneous load reduction.

→ Concept Map (SoL layer) · Science of Learning Basics

Engagement

Active investment of attention and effort — what learning requires from a student; not the same as compliance or quiet.

→ Concept Map (5.1) · 5.1 Behavior & Engagement

Expectations & Norms

Shared agreements that make engagement and culture possible — structural prerequisite for both strands (5.1 and 5.3).

→ Concept Map (5.1/5.3 bridge)

Feedback

How information about performance travels back to a student in ways that actually change what they do next. Requires a relationship to land.

→ Concept Map (5.1) · Science of Learning Basics

Motivation

Internal and external conditions that sustain or suppress engagement over time; mastery, autonomy, and belonging are the levers.

→ Concept Map (5.1)

Physical Space

The room’s arrangement as a designed condition — where students sit, where materials live, what the walls communicate or compete with.

→ Concept Map (5.2) · 5.2 Time, Space & Flow

Relationships

The foundational condition — the channel every correction, feedback, and repair has to travel through to land. Built first, not after.

→ Concept Map (5.3 hub) · 5.3 Culture & Belonging

Repair

What happens after harm — how a classroom community metabolizes conflict and returns to function without removing the people involved from it.

→ Concept Map (5.3) · Restorative Practices Basics

Routines

Repeated procedures that become automatic, freeing working memory for the actual learning. The difference between a procedure a class has been taught and one they are still figuring out.

→ Concept Map (5.2) · 5.2 Time, Space & Flow

Transitions

The movement between activities — predictable transitions reduce extraneous cognitive load; unpredictable ones spend working memory on navigation instead of content.

→ Concept Map (5.2) · 5.2 Time, Space & Flow

Visual Supports

Environmental anchors that reduce the need to hold information in working memory — schedules, anchor charts, procedure reminders.

→ Concept Map (5.2) · 5.2 Time, Space & Flow

Working Memory

The small, active store where thinking happens. Automaticity from rehearsed routines frees it for complex work. Holds roughly four chunks at a time; fatigues under extraneous load.

→ Concept Map (SoL layer) · Science of Learning Basics

The structural unit of SchoolHouse. Each major topic area is a room with its own frame, its own reading, its own reader. “Start with whichever one is closest to what’s on your mind.”

connect factor / fix factor

The load-bearing distinction in Attendance: a school can connect to a barrier condition — reduce friction, support the transition back — but cannot fix what sits in the household. “You connect; you don’t fix. Agency sits with the household at these bands.”

the return

What a student walks back into after an absence — the social, academic, and relational conditions waiting on the other side. “The school owns the return, not the illness.”
