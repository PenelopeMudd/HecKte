The disciplines concentrate where schools have the least agency, and go quiet where schools have the most.

What the shape says

The top half is crowded. The bottom half is nearly empty. Row 9 is empty entirely.

Factors 1–5 are where attendance is made possible — health, transport, money, caregiving. Six or seven fields converge on each, with mature instruments, funded interventions, and decades of literature.

Factors 6–9 are where attendance is chosen. Coverage thins to one or two marks, most of them partial, and stops.

That is the same finding as every discipline locates the cause outside the school building — reached from the opposite direction. The two lists were built separately, which is what makes the convergence worth something.

Row 9

Nothing studies it. Not thinly — not at all. Researchers measure engagement, connectedness, motivation, belonging, future orientation, relevance. Very few ask what a particular building’s lived value proposition is, and none take it as their question.

It is the row a school most controls. That pairing — highest design influence, zero disciplinary ownership — is why Factor 9 is this room’s Missing Measure.

Rows 6 and 7

Marked ○ under education and developmental psychology, and both marks are partial for the same reason: those fields study climate and relationship as outcomes or as clinical context, not as attendance mechanisms. School climate research exists and rarely joins itself to attendance data. Connecticut collects both and has not merged them.

These rows are covered enough to look owned and not enough to be answered.

What the grid does not settle

Whether nine consolidates to three or four. The grid suggests a seam — 1–5 read as one problem the school connects students to, 6–9 as another the school builds. That seam is the barrier/reason split already in the frame, and it is the most likely consolidation.
