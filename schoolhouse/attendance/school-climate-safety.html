School Climate & Safety

What is it like to be here, and does being here cost anything? This is the first factor whose answer is authored almost entirely inside the walls.

What is it

What happens in the hallway, the cafeteria, the bathroom, and the first ten minutes of a period — and whether the adults’ response to conflict makes a student more or less willing to walk in tomorrow.

This is where the school becomes the variable. The barriers ask what is happening in the student’s life. This one asks what is happening in this building. Climate is authored on site, by adults, and students read it long before any survey captures it.

Climate is the sum of many people’s ordinary decisions. The decisions that shape it — who supervises unowned space, what happens after a fight, whether a report goes anywhere — are made daily by people at every altitude, and they accumulate into precedent.

What did we learn

How big it is. The association between bullying, perceived safety, and attendance is widely reported and not causally established. What is established is the pattern: perceived safety tracks connectedness; connectedness tracks attendance. (Descriptive consensus across the literature.) Demonstrated as association.

What actually returns days. Klevan (2021) — whole-school restorative practice is associated with improved climate and connectedness and reduced exclusionary discipline; exclusionary discipline increases future misbehavior and dropout risk. (Synthesis brief — reports these findings rather than producing them.)

What Connecticut shows. Restorative practices legally defined in statute, with mandatory adoption by July 1, 2025, plus a mandatory school climate survey in 2025–26. Chronic absenteeism fell 20% → 17.7% in 2023–24. Working example — not a controlled demonstration. The climate survey produces connectedness data; the attendance reporting produces absence data. Nothing yet connects the two at state scale. The most direct available test of this factor exists as two datasets nobody has merged.

Honest limit: nothing in our sources isolates a climate change as the cause of an attendance change. A climate problem located in one class period leaves a student present in the building and absent from that room — a pattern an annual attendance figure reports as attendance. Treat this factor as structurally strong and empirically correlational.

What do we know so far

Climate is the standing record of how conflict gets handled here. Students are not surveying their feelings — they are reading precedent: what happened the last time something went wrong, to whom, and whether anyone repaired it. That record is what they consult at 6:45 in the morning.

The reachable person changes by band.

K–2

Agency sits with the household, so climate is read by the adult who brings the child. The climate that matters at this band is the climate of the entry — the door, the desk, the tone of the first contact. A caregiver who finds the office cold or the last phone call accusatory can decide the trip isn’t worth it.

3–5

Peer safety becomes legible. The child can now name what happened at recess and can produce a stomachache the morning after. Unowned space starts doing the damage — the playground, the line, the bus — because that is where the adult pattern is thinnest.

6–8

The student can now decline. Attendance commonly falls across the elementary–middle transition, and this is the band where avoiding a person becomes a reason to avoid a building. Named adults in unowned spaces, and a conflict report that visibly produces something, are the moves.

9–12

The student prices the day. Climate now competes with a shift, a sibling, a bed. A building that costs something to enter loses that comparison quickly. Partial-day absence is the tell — she is in the building and not in the room, which reads on the record as present.

Emerging questions

  • The Connecticut climate survey and chronic absenteeism data exist. What happens when they’re merged?

The boundary

You cannot prevent conflict. You cannot eliminate social friction or make a hallway feel safe by announcing that it should. The social conditions are not fully yours.

What you own is what happens after conflict. Who supervises the unowned spaces. Whether a report goes anywhere. The precedent that accumulates from ordinary decisions made daily is climate — and you are making those decisions whether or not you are thinking of them that way.

Climate is authored here. The claim this factor makes is not that you are responsible for everything — it is that the record of ordinary decisions belongs to the building.
