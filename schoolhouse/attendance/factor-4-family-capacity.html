Family Capacity & Caregiving

Does the household have the bandwidth to execute a school morning? This is the factor most likely to be misread as a judgment about parents. It is a reading of load, not of care.

The participation question

Can this household run the morning? Not does this family value education. Whether the sequence — wake, feed, dress, transport, arrive — can be executed by the adults available, on a shift schedule they do not set, with the health and the vehicle and the other children they actually have.

What you can do here — and what you can’t

Attendance is a requirement the school cannot produce. The morning happens in a house. The single decision that determines attendance at the lower bands is made by an adult the school may never have met.

What the school owns is the nature of its contact with that adult. Every school has contact with households. Almost all of it is triggered by a problem and takes the form of a summons. That is a design choice, and it is revisable.

ObserveWhich households are reachable and which are not · who has a caregiver working nights · who is the eldest of several · address changes and the absences that follow them
InfluenceWhether a specific adult at the school knows a specific caregiver · whether the family has any non-summoning contact · whether contact happens in a language the household uses
RedesignHow a family is contacted — trigger, tone, medium, and who does it · whether the first contact of a year is ever positive · what an absence notification actually says · office hours set against shift work

You cannot give a household more hours, a second adult, a day shift, or a working car. The morning happens in a house you do not enter.

What you own is what that adult has heard from this school. Whether they have ever received anything from you that was not a notification of a failure.

A caregiver with one warm contact here has somewhere to bring a problem. That is a smaller claim than “family engagement,” and it is one a single person can keep.

Read through Restorative Practices

(This room’s lens. See Restorative Practices — Basics.)

The agreement is with the household, and this is the factor where that is most literal. At the lower bands the school’s attendance agreement is not with the student at all. It is an agreement between the building and a caregiver, and it holds or lapses on the terms of that relationship.

The escalation, named: truancy law’s response to a household that cannot execute the morning is a court date that takes the caregiver off work. Sanctions on a household already failing to hold an agreement make holding it harder.

By grade band

The household holds everything, and this factor is at maximum weight. A six-year-old’s attendance rate is a direct measurement of a household’s operational capacity that week. There is no student-side variable to work with at all.

Still household-executed, and the child begins to have a role in the household’s operation. Walking a younger sibling, getting themselves ready so a caregiver can leave. Some students at this band are already load-bearing.

The handoff, and it goes badly when the household is under load. The student can now decline, and a caregiver stretched thin may not have the capacity to override it.

The student may be part of the household’s caregiving capacity, not a recipient of it. Watching siblings, translating, driving a parent to work, holding a shift.

Evidence

Connecticut — LEAP, a relationship-building home-visiting program. Chronic absenteeism fell 20% → 17.7% in 2023–24, with the greatest gains in the early grades. A working example, not a controlled demonstration.

Wraparound case management — coordination across housing, health, income, and legal services — is the intervention most consistently associated with reduced chronic absence in the highest-barrier populations. Demonstrated as association.

Honest limit: the family-capacity literature is descriptive. It establishes what conditions co-occur with absence and does not establish that any school-side change in family contact alters a household’s capacity to produce a school morning.
