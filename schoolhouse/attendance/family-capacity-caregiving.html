Family Capacity & Caregiving

Does the household have the bandwidth to execute a school morning?

What is it

Whether the sequence — wake, feed, dress, transport, arrive — can be executed by the adults available, on a schedule they do not set, with the vehicle and children they actually have.

The morning happens in a house the school does not enter. The single decision that determines attendance at the lower grades is made by an adult the school may never have met. What the school holds is the nature of its contact with that adult — every contact is either a summons or something else, and almost all of it is a summons.

What did we learn

How big it is. Household conditions that co-occur with absence: shift work conflicting with school schedules, caregiver health crises, multiple residential moves, language barriers, and the burden of managing a child’s chronic illness inside an already-stressed household. (Family systems lens, convergent discipline analysis.) Housing instability is associated with elevated parenting stress, reduced social support, and lower school engagement across multiple studies in the family systems literature.

What actually returns days. Connecticut’s LEAP — a relationship-building home-visiting program running since 2021 in the districts with the highest chronic absenteeism — ran alongside restorative practice written into statute (mandatory July 2025). Chronic absenteeism fell 20% → 17.7% in 2023–24, with the greatest gains in the early grades. (Working example, not controlled demonstration — nobody has isolated the home-visiting contribution.)

What the contact history carries. If every contact a household has received has been a notification of a deficit, the relationship has one register, and it is adversarial. A caregiver with one warm, non-summoning contact at the school has somewhere to bring a problem. A caregiver whose only contact is a letter counting days has somewhere to avoid.

What nobody knows. That changing how a school contacts families changes attendance — Connecticut is the closest thing and it has several moving parts, and nobody has untangled what the contact relationship contributed to the result.

What do we know so far

The truancy response to a household that cannot execute the morning is a court date that takes the caregiver off work. Sanctions on a household already failing to hold an agreement make holding it harder. The enforcement mechanism removes the capacity it is trying to restore.

The reachable person changes by band.

K–2

A six-year-old’s attendance rate is a direct measurement of a household’s operational capacity that week. There is no student-side variable. Agency sits with the caregiver, entirely — which makes the relationship with that adult the intervention rather than a preliminary to it.

3–5

Still household-executed, and the child begins to have a role in the household’s operation — walking a younger sibling, getting themselves ready so a caregiver can leave. Some students at this band are already load-bearing. What a household believes about this building in third grade is what it brings to sixth.

6–8

The handoff goes badly under load. The student can now decline, and a caregiver stretched thin may not have the capacity to override it. Household capacity and student choice start compounding — a stressed household produces less morning enforcement exactly as the student gains the ability to refuse.

9–12

The student may be part of the household’s caregiving capacity, not a recipient of it — watching siblings, translating, driving a parent to work, holding a shift. Absence at this band is frequently a student meeting the household’s obligations rather than avoiding the school’s. Routing contact through the household may be exactly what breaks the relationship.

Emerging questions

  • If household contact history shapes caregiver willingness, how early does the relationship need to start — and what does it need to contain?
