Relationships & Belonging

Is there anyone here who would notice they were gone?

What is it

Whether a specific person in this building holds a specific student’s absence as a specific loss — not a data point, a loss — and whether the student knows it.

The return is the mechanism. A student who comes back to good to see you and nothing else has learned that coming back costs nothing.

What did we learn

How big it is. Belonging conditions motivation — students engage where they feel accepted, and belonging functions as a precondition, not a reward. (Deans for Impact, Science of Learning 2nd ed., Q4.) Exclusionary discipline increases future misbehavior and dropout risk. (Klevan (2021) — synthesis brief.)

What actually returns days. Connecticut’s LEAP — relationship-building home visits in the districts with the highest chronic absenteeism, running since 2021, alongside restorative practice written into statute (mandatory July 2025). Chronic absenteeism fell 20% → 17.7% in 2023–24, with the greatest gains in the early grades. (Working example, not controlled demonstration.)

What making return cheap does. The restorative question is what happened, not why weren’t you here — the second invites a defense. A student who returns to a demand for justification learns that coming back costs something. Absence generates its own next absence, and it does so through the return, not through the days themselves.

What nobody knows. Nothing in our sources isolates “a student had one trusted adult” as a cause of attendance. What exists is consistent association across climate, connectedness, and attendance, plus one state-scale working example. Treat this as well-supported and not proven.

What do we know so far

Absence is a lapsed agreement, not a violation. The school requires attendance and can only obtain it by consent. A student who stops coming has withdrawn from an agreement — sometimes because the conditions collapsed, sometimes deliberately. Either reading calls for repair, not enforcement.

The reachable person changes by band.

K–2

The relationship that matters is with the household. A six-year-old has no say in whether they arrive. A caregiver who has one warm, non-summoning contact at the school is more likely to get a child there than one whose only contact has been a letter about absences. Connecticut’s greatest gains were in kindergarten and early grades — the bands where household agency is total.

3–5

Both, and the child begins to have preferences. The relationship with the household still executes arrival; the relationship with the child determines whether they want to be delivered. Roles in the room’s operation start to matter — a student who runs something is expected in a way the attendance record does not capture.

6–8

The student can now decline. Social reasons become primary: who they would face, what they would have to explain about being gone. The move is making return cheap — someone who says good to see you and nothing else, before asking anything.

9–12

A job, siblings to watch, a calculation that the day isn’t worth what it costs. One adult who knows her situation and does not moralize about it is the version that works here. The bar is that this building contains at least one person for whom her absence is a specific loss.
