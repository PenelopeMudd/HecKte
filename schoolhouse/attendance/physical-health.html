Physical Health

Is the body able to be here today — and what happens when it wasn’t?

What is it

Absence caused by the body: acute illness, chronic conditions like asthma and diabetes, dental pain, injury, and the appointments that go with them.

Mental and behavioral health is a separate factor, though the two arrive together often enough that a single stomachache can belong to both.

The illness itself sits outside the building — diagnosis, treatment, insurance, whether the family has a pediatrician at all. What the school holds is the return.

The widest gap in this room’s evidence is by disability — 14.8% health-related chronic absence among children with disabilities against 4.4% without. (NCHS 2024)

What did we learn

How big it is. Around 3 million children missed 15 or more school days for health reasons in 2022. (CDC) Asthma is the most commonly cited single condition, at roughly 13–14 million missed days a year. The AAP establishes the relationship as bidirectional — poor health causes absence, and chronic absence predicts worse long-term health.

What actually returns days. Home-based multi-trigger asthma intervention returns about 12.3 school days per student per year. (Community Preventive Services Task Force, 2011) It worked, and it happened in the home. The school’s part was the connection to it.

What happens once she’s back. Excusing an absence doesn’t reduce its cost — Gottfried (2009) was the first to separate excused from unexcused rigorously, and found both lowering achievement. And the nurse’s office is already doing relational work: Lee (2023) found students with frequent partial-day absences seeking out the school nurse for comfort and support, whether or not any building designed it that way.

What nobody knows. The literature is strong on how many days illness removes and quiet on whether any school practice changes a health-related attendance trajectory.

What do we know so far

What a student walks back into is a set of decisions someone made and wrote down: whether five days of work arrive at once, whether coming back requires a note, whether the first question is what happened or where were you.

The reachable person changes by band.

K–2

The caregiver decides everything: whether today is a stay-home day, whether the appointment gets made. A caregiver with one non-summoning contact at the school has somewhere to ask. One whose only contact is a letter counting days does not.

3–5

The child starts producing symptoms, and many are genuinely both real and school-triggered. Sorting them isn’t possible and isn’t the job. What the building controls is whether the nurse’s office can be visited without it becoming an event.

6–8

The student begins managing their own body in front of peers. An inhaler in a hallway, a pass that comes too often. Concealment and use both happen, often in the same student.

9–12

Illness becomes the reason offered more often than the reason operating, because it’s the one the system accepts. Reading that as deception misses what it is.

Emerging questions

  • What explains the disability gap — the conditions themselves, the appointments they require, or how schools handle repeated absence in students already flagged?
  • If illness is the only uncontested way to cover an absence, how does that affect the flattening of the concept?
