Meaning, Identity & Value

Why is this school worth attending? This is the room’s Missing Measure. The least-developed area in the absenteeism literature is the one almost entirely within school design. Everyone knows it operates. No discipline owns it. No instrument captures it. Named here as a proposition, not a finding.

The participation question

What does this building give her that nowhere else does? Not is she motivated — that locates the question inside the student, which is the move this whole project is diagnosing. Whether this particular school, as lived, has a value proposition she could state. And whether it holds when she is sixteen and something else is paying her.

What you can do here — and what you can’t

Attendance is a requirement the school cannot produce. But this factor is the inverse of the barriers: almost none of it is outside the building. Whether a school is worth attending is authored on site, by adults, in the ordinary arrangement of a day.

Which is also why it is unheld. (Whose job is it to make a child feel this building is a place where they matter? The answer seems to be everyone — which often means no one.) A responsibility distributed to everyone has no owner, and this one behaves accordingly.

ObserveWhat a student would name if asked what she comes for · who has an answer and who does not · what is here that exists nowhere else in her week
InfluenceWhether a student has something in the building that is hers · whether the day contains anything she would miss · whether an adult can name what this school offers her specifically
RedesignWhat a day is arranged around · whether students hold real roles with real consequence · whether anything runs that would visibly fail without her · what the school claims it is for, and whether the schedule agrees

What sits outside this factor: the household, the ride, the body, the money. Those are real and usually larger. You connect students to those; other systems resolve them. What you cannot do is supply meaning by assertion — a value proposition announced is not a value proposition experienced, and students audit the difference immediately.

You cannot produce attendance, and you cannot hand a student a reason to want this. Meaning is not deliverable; it is only arrangeable.

What you can do is make sure something in this building would actually fail without her, and that she knows what it is.

That is a smaller claim than any engagement initiative makes, and it is one you can actually keep.

Read through Restorative Practices

(This room’s lens. See Restorative Practices — Basics.)

Attendance is met by agreement, and an agreement needs something on both sides. The school requires attendance and cannot produce it; it can only obtain it by consent. Consent to what, in exchange for what — that is this factor. Factors 6 and 7 make the agreement bearable and personal. This one is whether there is anything in it for her.

Which is why enforcement reads as bad faith. Truancy law treats a consent problem as a compliance problem. Sanctioning a student for declining an exchange she was never offered terms on is the clearest version of the pattern: the requirement-holder’s shortfall, assigned to the party least able to contest it.

Restorative practice supplies the missing half — a role. Its core mechanic is that a person has a part in repairing what broke and a route back into the community. Generalized past conflict, that is a standing offer of standing: you are someone here whose participation has consequence. A student who holds a real role in how this place runs has an answer to why come that no attendance campaign produces.

And identity is the version that survives the barriers. This is a place where I am someone outlasts this is a place I enjoy, because it is not contingent on any given day being good.

By grade band

Attendance agency moves from the household into the student. This factor changes hands as it moves.

Agency is entirely the household’s, so the value proposition is the household’s to evaluate. A six-year-old does not weigh whether school is worth it. The caregiver does — and the honest reading at this band is that the value proposition is largely custodial and social: care during working hours, food, a safe room, other children.

That is not a lesser answer. It is a real one, and naming it plainly is more useful than dressing it up. The design question at K–2 is whether the household experiences the school as a place that is good to their child, which is assembled almost entirely from the tone of ordinary contact.

Both, and the child forms a first identity claim about school. I am good at this. I have a job here. They need me for the thing on Fridays. These are small and they are the beginning of a durable answer.

Household agency still executes arrival. What changes is that the child now has a reason of her own, and it is portable — it survives a bad morning in a way that enjoyment does not.

The handoff, and the question turns explicit. The student can decline, and this is the band where why am I doing this is asked out loud for the first time. Belonging and meaning separate here: she can have friends in the building and still find no reason to be in it.

The move is something real that is hers — a role, a team, an instrument, a job, a responsibility that visibly does not happen if she is gone. Not an incentive. An incentive is something the school gives her for coming; a role is something that fails without her.

The student decides, and this factor is competing directly. A shift pays. A sibling needs watching. She is running an explicit comparison, and the school is one option among several that all have real returns.

The version that works here is a route she can see. Not school matters — a specific line from this building to something she wants, that she believes exists and believes she can walk. Where that line is real, she attends through significant barriers. Where it is not, no relationship compensates for very long, because she is not wrong about the arithmetic.

Evidence

This is the thinnest evidence base of the nine factors, and the thinness is itself the finding. What follows is stated at the strength it actually has.

The gap is documented by its absence. The literature measures engagement, connectedness, motivation, belonging, future orientation, and relevance. Very little of it asks directly: why is this school worth attending? What is the lived value proposition of this particular building? No named study in this room’s evidence base takes that as its question. (Kim’s contribution — the Missing Measure claim for this room. Not a finding drawn from the literature; a claim about what the literature does not contain.)

Gottfried (2014) — chronic absence lowers socioemotional outcomes including educational and social engagement, not only test scores. (Causal, on a nationally representative kindergarten cohort.) The closest available adjacent evidence — it establishes that engagement moves with attendance, and not that a school’s value proposition moves engagement.

Balfanz & Byrnes (2012) — chronic absenteeism predicts dropout better than test scores or grades. Consistent with a student running a cost-benefit before she formally leaves; not evidence of what she is weighing.

RAND (2024) — Districts Try with Limited Success to Reduce Chronic Absenteeism, RRA956-26. The standard toolkit is being run and the aggregate is not moving. RAND cannot say why — bad interventions, bad implementation, or the wrong target. One available reading is that the toolkit addresses barriers and messaging while leaving the exchange itself untouched. That reading is a hypothesis, not RAND’s conclusion.

Honest limit — the largest in this room. No study establishes that a school’s value proposition affects attendance, because no study has been found that measures the value proposition at all. The grade-band claims above are reasoned from the developmental gradient and from Kim’s band-dependence work; they are not empirically established at any band. The 9–12 claim in particular — that a visible route outperforms relationship when the two diverge — is a proposition, and stating it as anything else would repeat the error this project exists to name. Treat this factor as structurally the strongest and evidentially the weakest of the nine. It is here because it is the piece a school most controls, not because it is the piece best demonstrated.
