Meaning, Identity & Value

Why is this school worth attending? This is the room’s Missing Measure. The least-developed area in the absenteeism literature is the one almost entirely within school design. Everyone knows it operates. No discipline owns it. Named here as a proposition, not a finding.

The participation question

What does this building give her that nowhere else does? Not is she motivated. Whether this particular school, as lived, has a value proposition she could state.

What you can do here — and what you can’t

Attendance is a requirement the school cannot produce. But this factor is the inverse of the barriers: almost none of it is outside the building. Whether a school is worth attending is authored on site, by adults, in the ordinary arrangement of a day.

ObserveWhat a student would name if asked what she comes for · who has an answer and who does not · what is here that exists nowhere else in her week
InfluenceWhether a student has something in the building that is hers · whether the day contains anything she would miss · whether an adult can name what this school offers her specifically
RedesignWhat a day is arranged around · whether students hold real roles with real consequence · whether anything runs that would visibly fail without her · what the school claims it is for, and whether the schedule agrees

You cannot produce attendance, and you cannot hand a student a reason to want this. Meaning is not deliverable; it is only arrangeable.

What you can do is make sure something in this building would actually fail without her, and that she knows what it is.

That is a smaller claim than any engagement initiative makes, and it is one you can actually keep.

Read through Restorative Practices

(This room’s lens. See Restorative Practices — Basics.)

Attendance is met by agreement, and an agreement needs something on both sides. Factors 6 and 7 make the agreement bearable and personal. This one is whether there is anything in it for her.

Restorative practice supplies the missing half — a role. A student who holds a real role in how this place runs has an answer to why come that no attendance campaign produces.

By grade band

Agency is entirely the household’s, so the value proposition is the household’s to evaluate. The honest reading at this band is that the value proposition is largely custodial and social: care during working hours, food, a safe room, other children. That is not a lesser answer. It is a real one.

Both, and the child forms a first identity claim about school. I am good at this. I have a job here. They need me for the thing on Fridays. These are small and they are the beginning of a durable answer.

The handoff, and the question turns explicit. The move is something real that is hers — a role, a team, an instrument, a job, a responsibility that visibly does not happen if she is gone. Not an incentive. An incentive is something the school gives her for coming; a role is something that fails without her.

The student decides, and this factor is competing directly. The version that works here is a route she can see — a specific line from this building to something she wants, that she believes exists and believes she can walk.

Evidence

This is the thinnest evidence base of the nine factors, and the thinness is itself the finding. No named study in this room’s evidence base takes the value proposition question as its question.

Gottfried (2014) — chronic absence lowers socioemotional outcomes including educational and social engagement. The closest available adjacent evidence.

RAND (2024) — districts are running the standard toolkit and absenteeism is not moving. One available reading: the toolkit addresses barriers while leaving the exchange itself untouched.

Honest limit — the largest in this room. No study establishes that a school’s value proposition affects attendance. Treat this factor as structurally the strongest and evidentially the weakest of the nine.
