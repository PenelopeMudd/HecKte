Where each factor stands

Factor 7 — Relationships & Belonging. Consistent association across climate, connectedness, and attendance, plus Connecticut as a state-scale working example. No study isolates “a student had one trusted adult” as a cause of attendance. Demonstrated as association; unproven as mechanism.

Factor 6 — School Climate & Safety. Klevan (2021) reports the climate and exclusionary-discipline associations rather than producing them. The specific claims — bullying tracks absenteeism, fair discipline improves attendance — come from the field’s descriptive consensus. Structurally strong, empirically correlational.

Factor 8 — Academic Experience. Gottfried (2014) is causal on what absence does: it lowers achievement and socioemotional outcomes, and it spills over onto classmates who attend. Gottfried is explicitly quiet on what causes absence. The direction this factor rests on — that academic difficulty produces absence — is assumed field-wide and tested by nothing in this evidence base. Reasoned, not demonstrated.

Factor 9 — Meaning, Identity & Value. No study establishes that a school’s value proposition affects attendance, because no study has been found that measures the value proposition at all. The grade-band claims are reasoned from the developmental gradient. Proposed. It is here because it is the piece a school most controls.

The assumptions underneath all of them

That attendance is a requirement met by agreement. Every input to a child arriving happens outside the building. This is the load-bearing assumption; the room’s structure follows from it.

That participation and presence are different things, and that a school-based reader can affect the second more than the first. This is a judgment about where agency sits, supported by the developmental gradient and by which interventions have worked where.

That agency moves from household to student across the grade bands. A six-year-old has no say in whether they arrive; a seventeen-year-old decides. Every factor row changes hands along that axis, which is why the room is banded rather than general. Supported by developmental reasoning and consistent with where band-differentiated programs have shown results. Reasoned.

That restorative practice is the right lens. Chosen because it fits the shape of a problem obtained by consent, and because Connecticut organized a state system this way and attendance moved. Nobody has isolated restorative practice’s contribution. A working example, not a controlled demonstration.

That the nine factors are a starting frame. Nine is where the reading landed, not a finding — the room calls this the Iteration of Nine and holds it while building. See the cross-tab.

What the measures can carry

Missouri’s Proportional Attendance Rate is hour-based — hours attended ÷ hours enrolled — and reports one annual percentage against a 90% threshold. Half-days, early releases, and virtual participation resolve correctly.

What the published figure carries forward is a single number per student. A student at 89% and a student at 50% appear the same. The pattern — consecutive days, every Monday, first period only — stays inside the district. Districts hold the hour-level detail that produces the state figure; the summary is what gets published.

Denominators move for reasons unrelated to attendance. One Missouri district’s rate fell from 17.4% to 5.2% in a year because a virtual academy entered its enrollment. No child’s attendance changed.

The federal measure is a different instrument. EDFacts chronic absenteeism counts whole days. It and PAR are not interchangeable and should never be blended in one series.

What sits outside this room

Most of what determines whether a student attends — transportation, health, housing, household capacity — is beyond any school’s reach. This room is deliberately smaller than the problem.

Naming that boundary is the point rather than a limitation. Nine disciplines were consulted, and every one located the cause outside the school building while measuring the child. A room that claimed the whole problem would repeat exactly that: assigning a shortfall to whoever is nearest and least able to contest it.

How to argue with this room

  • Where a claim is marked Reasoned or Proposed, the honest response is to test it, and this room would rather be corrected than cited.
  • The strongest available test of Factor 6 already exists as two unmerged datasets — Connecticut’s mandatory climate survey and its attendance reporting. Nothing found so far connects them.
  • The residual method — predicting attendance from structural conditions and studying the schools that beat their prediction — is designed and waits on school-level attendance data.
