Meaning, Identity & Value

Why is this school worth attending?

What is it

Whether this building, as lived, has a value proposition the student could state — and whether it holds when she is sixteen and something else is paying her.

The literature measures engagement, connectedness, motivation, belonging, and future orientation. Almost none of it asks directly what the value proposition of a particular building is — the gap in the instruments is itself the finding. (Kim’s structural contribution — a claim about what the literature does not contain.)

What did we learn

How big it is. Gottfried (2014) — chronic absence lowers socioemotional outcomes including educational and social engagement. (Causal, nationally representative kindergarten cohort.) This is the closest adjacent finding — it establishes that engagement moves with attendance. It does not establish that a building’s value proposition moves engagement.

What actually returns days. Balfanz & Byrnes (2012) — chronic absenteeism predicts dropout better than test scores or grades. (Johns Hopkins.) Consistent with a student running a cost-benefit before she formally leaves. Not evidence of what she is weighing.

What RAND found. RAND (2024) — districts running the standard toolkit are not reducing chronic absenteeism at the rate the effort would predict. (RRA956-26.) One available reading: the toolkit addresses barriers and messaging while leaving the exchange itself — what the school offers in return for attendance — untouched. That reading is a hypothesis, not RAND’s conclusion.

What nobody knows. Nothing in our sources establishes that a school’s value proposition affects attendance, because nothing measures the value proposition at all. This factor is structurally the strongest and evidentially the weakest of the nine.

What do we know so far

Meaning is not deliverable; it is only arrangeable. A value proposition announced is not one experienced, and students audit the difference immediately. The difference between an incentive and a role: an incentive is something the school gives her for coming; a role is something that fails without her.

The reachable person changes by band.

K–2

Agency is entirely the household’s, so the value proposition is the household’s to evaluate. The honest reading at this band is that the value is largely custodial and social — care during working hours, food, a safe room, other children. The design question is whether the household experiences the school as a place that is good to their child, assembled almost entirely from the tone of ordinary contact.

3–5

Both, and the child forms a first identity claim about school. I am good at this. I have a job here. They need me for the thing on Fridays. These are small, and they are the beginning of a durable answer. What changes is that the child now has a reason of her own — one that is portable and survives a bad morning in a way that enjoyment does not.

6–8

The student can decline, and this is the band where why am I doing this is asked out loud for the first time. Belonging and meaning separate here: she can have friends in the building and still find no reason to be in it. Something real that is hers — a role, a team, an instrument, a job that does not happen if she is absent — is what works at this band.

9–12

A shift pays. A sibling needs watching. She is running an explicit comparison, and the school is one option among several with real returns. A specific line from this building to something she wants — that she believes exists and believes she can walk — is what keeps her. Where that line is real, she attends through significant barriers. Where it is not, no relationship compensates for long.
