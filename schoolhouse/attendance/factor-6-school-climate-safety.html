School Climate & Safety

What is it like to be here, and does being here cost anything? This is where the school becomes the variable. The barriers ask what is happening in the student’s life. This one asks what is happening in this building — and it is the first factor whose answer is authored almost entirely inside the walls.

The participation question

Is a day here survivable? Not does the student feel safe as a sentiment. Whether the hallway, the cafeteria, the bathroom, and the first ten minutes of a period are places she can enter without paying a price.

What you can do here — and what you can’t

Attendance is a requirement the school cannot produce. But climate is different from the other factors: the conditions are manufactured on site. Who supervises the hallway, what happens after a fight, whether a report goes anywhere — those are decisions, and decisions have authors.

ObserveWhere students slow down or route around · who eats alone · which periods generate the nurse visits and the bathroom passes · who leaves after third hour and comes back tomorrow
InfluenceWhether a reported conflict produces a response the student can see · whether the adult response to a hard moment is repair or removal · whether the same behavior gets the same answer from you on a bad day
RedesignSupervision of unowned space — halls, cafeteria, bus line · what happens after a conflict · re-entry after suspension · transition-year structures at the 5→6 and 8→9 seams

You cannot produce attendance, and you do not own the building’s climate. Nobody in the building owns all of it.

What you own is your share of the precedent. What happens in your room when something goes wrong, and what a student walks back into afterward.

That is a smaller claim than most climate initiatives make, and it is one you can actually keep.

Read through Restorative Practices

(This room’s lens. See Restorative Practices — Basics.)

Climate is the standing record of how conflict gets handled here. Students are not surveying their feelings. They are reading precedent: what happened the last time something went wrong, to whom, and whether anyone repaired it.

Safety and accountability are the same commitment, not a trade. A building that does only accountability is unsafe. A building that does only safety is where absence starts.

By grade band

Agency sits with the household, so climate is read by the adult who brings the child. The climate that matters at this band is the climate of the entry — the door, the desk, the tone of the first contact.

Both, and peer safety becomes legible. Unowned space starts doing the damage — the playground, the line, the bus — because that is where the adult pattern is thinnest.

The handoff, and the transition seam. The student can now decline. The move is shrinking the unowned space: named adults on the hall, on the bus line, in the cafeteria.

The student decides, and she prices the day. Partial-day absence is the tell here — she is in the building and not in the room.

Evidence

Klevan (2021) — whole-school restorative practice is associated with improved climate and connectedness and reduced exclusionary discipline; exclusionary discipline increases future misbehavior and dropout risk.

Connecticut — restorative practices legally defined in statute, mandatory as of July 1, 2025. Chronic absenteeism fell 20% → 17.7% in 2023–24. A working example, not a controlled demonstration.

Honest limit: the association between bullying, perceived safety, and attendance is widely reported and not causally established. Treat this factor as structurally strong and empirically correlational.
