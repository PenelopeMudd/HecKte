Repair

What you do after harm — the response that addresses the rupture rather than the person.

After harm, the impulse is to resolve it quickly: remove the student, assign a consequence, move on. But removal is precisely the response that makes the next interaction harder. The student leaves knowing they’re in trouble, not knowing what they’d do differently, and returns to a room they’ve been expelled from. Repair is slower, harder to fit into the schedule, and the only response that actually addresses the damage rather than the symptom.

Repair requires both relationship and the capacity for redirection — you can’t repair with a student you don’t know, and you can’t repair a harm that was never addressed directly. The sequence matters: the incident gets named, the impact gets named, and the path forward gets built together rather than assigned unilaterally.
