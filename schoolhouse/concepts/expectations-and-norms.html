Expectations & Norms

The agreement a class operates under — and the difference between knowing a rule and holding one.

Clarity about expectations is necessary but not sufficient. A student can know the rule and still not hold it — knowing isn’t the same as owning. The distinction between a student who follows a norm and one who holds it is authorship: students who helped write the agreement follow it because it’s theirs, not because someone made it. Posted rules produce compliance. Co-authored agreements produce investment.

This concept bridges the behavioral and cultural strands because it does both jobs at once: it’s the floor for managing behavior, and it’s also a belonging move. A class that co-created its norms has done something together. That’s a shared artifact, which is a different kind of thing than a list on the wall.

What has to be in place

Nothing — this is a foundation.
