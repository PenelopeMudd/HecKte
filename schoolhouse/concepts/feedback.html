Missouri Standard 5.1 · Behavior & Engagement

Feedback

Information about performance that a student can actually use — which requires it to arrive in a form they can receive.

Feedback is theoretically one of the highest-leverage tools a teacher has. In practice, it often fails before it arrives. Being right isn’t sufficient. Feedback aimed at a person requires that person to hold their own standing while processing it, which they cannot do simultaneously. What gets received as useful information depends heavily on how it’s delivered, and on what already exists between the teacher and the student. The same sentence lands as help or as judgment depending on the relationship it travels through.

This is also why feedback-as-commentary-on-the-person fails so consistently: there’s no channel for it. Feedback aimed at the work, with the student and the teacher both looking at the same thing, sidesteps that problem by design.
