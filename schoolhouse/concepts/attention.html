Attention

What the mind selects to process at any moment — captured, not commanded.

Attention is captured by whatever is most salient in the environment, not directed by what the teacher points to. A student in a room with uncertain expectations, unpredictable transitions, or unresolved social stress is spending attention on those things — and the lesson is competing with all of them simultaneously. The teacher who has to call the room back to attention has already lost some of it.

Predictable structure protects attention by making the environment legible. A student who knows what comes next, how to get materials, and what the norms are in this room can allocate attention to the content rather than to figuring out the room. Clean transitions are one of the highest-leverage moments: how a move is handled either recaptures attention or fragments it for the next ten minutes.
