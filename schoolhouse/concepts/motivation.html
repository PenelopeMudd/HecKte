Missouri Standard 5.1 · Behavior & Engagement

Motivation

The internal and external conditions that sustain or suppress engagement over time — mastery, autonomy, and belonging are the levers, not rewards.

Motivation is often treated as something a teacher has to wait for or produce in students — “the unmotivated student.” The learning-sciences reading runs the other direction: competence precedes motivation, not the reverse. A student who has evidence they can do something will want to do more of it. The implication is that the class you’re trying to engage first needs to succeed first, which requires structuring early experiences for success rather than for natural selection.

Extrinsic rewards reliably displace intrinsic motivation when the reward is removed. Autonomy, mastery, and belonging are the conditions that sustain it. None of those three is something you hand a student — they’re built into how the room is structured.
