Visual Supports

The specific visual, referencing taught content, available at the moment of need — not a print-rich environment.

The evidence supports a specific move: a visual referencing taught content, available at the moment of need, reduces cognitive load. It does not support a print-rich environment, which is what most classroom implementations produce instead. A room where everything is posted is a room where nothing is visible — every item added competes for the same attentional resources, and the aggregate load increases rather than decreases.

The distinction is selective vs. additive. A posted routine that a student can look at instead of asking you is a support. A wall of anchor charts, most of which reference content from three units ago, is an environment. The mechanism only works in the first case.
