Behavior & Redirections

What redirection looks like when it works — naming the deed, not the doer; the difference between correction and punishment.

The instinct when a student misbehaves is to correct and, if needed, remove — to address the symptom cleanly and quickly. But correction spends relational credit, and removal spends it faster. A student corrected without an existing relationship hears an accusation; a student removed hears that they don’t belong here, which is precisely the condition that generates the next incident. The redirect that lands is the one that can travel through a relationship that already exists.

Most behavior problems that read as defiance are actually one of three things: unclear expectations, missing skills, or a broken relationship. The correction doesn’t address any of those — it only applies a cost to the symptom, while the source continues.
