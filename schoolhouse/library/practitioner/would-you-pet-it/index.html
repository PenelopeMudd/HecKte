  • A baby fawn has almost no smell and lies frozen-still in the grass, so hunters walk right past it.

  • Stays all winter — it does not migrate.
  • Eats seeds, berries, and bugs all year long.
  • It is against the law to catch or keep a wild cardinal.
  • It will chase a hawk or a cat many times its size away from its nest without backing down.

  • Wings are covered in tiny scales like colored dust — touch them and the scales rub off.
  • It saves up poison from the milkweed it ate as a caterpillar, so a bird that tries one never tries another.

  • Lives in forests and fields in Missouri.
  • Eats worms, berries, and bugs.
  • Knows one small patch of woods its whole life — move it far and it will spend the rest of its life trying to get back.
  • It snaps its shell shut like a door with a hinge — pulled all the way in, nothing can reach it.

From Other Places

tap to flip
  • Lives in the Arctic, near the North Pole.
  • Hunts seals on sea ice to survive.
  • Needs cold — without ice, it can’t live.
  • Weighs as much as a car.
  • Its fur isn’t really white; it’s clear and hollow, so the bear seems to disappear against the snow.

  • Lives in Africa and Asia, near rivers and grasslands.
  • Eats up to 660 pounds of food every day.
  • Its skin looks tough but is actually sensitive to the sun — rolls in mud to protect it.
  • When danger comes, the grown-ups make a ring around the babies with the little ones tucked safe in the middle.
