Missouri Standard 5.3 · Relationship Building

Restorative Interactions

Two minutes of non-academic conversation with one hard-to-reach student, held for ten consecutive school days. The goal is not the ten conversations themselves; the goal is building a durable relationship with a student who is currently reachable only through the deed, not the person. Known in the field as 2×10.

What problem does this solve?

  • A student who feels unseen, or only seen when in trouble
  • Redirections that keep spending the bond instead of building it
  • A relationship that starts and stays in the negative
  • Escalating behavior with no relational channel to reach through

Why does it work?

The effect comes from the accumulation and the non-academic framing, not from any single conversation. Potential mechanisms include:

  • Ten consecutive days signals reliability — the student learns the adult keeps coming back. The consecutiveness matters more than the two minutes.
  • Non-academic content keeps the contact from reading as correction in disguise. The discipline is not steering the conversation toward school.
  • Repeated positive contact builds relationship before the next hard moment — so later redirection can land without spending the bond.
  • Belonging conditions motivation; students engage more where they feel accepted. The channel that feedback and instruction travel on gets stronger.

What conditions must exist?

  • The conversations stay genuinely non-academic — no schoolwork, no behavior talk.
  • The teacher can protect two low-stakes minutes daily (arrival, transition, dismissal).
  • Consistency across all ten days — a broken streak resets the signal.
  • The teacher is curious about this student, not gathering leverage.
  • Contact stays private and low-pressure — the student is not put on the spot publicly.

The common misconception is that 2×10 is a behavior-fix trick. The actual design principle: build the relationship before you need it, on the student's terms, not the classroom's.

By grade band

Two minutes of unhurried attention inside the existing day — arrival, snack, lining up. Talk about what they bring up, not what you'd choose. Young students will tell you about their life immediately if the attention is genuinely without an agenda; the discipline is not steering it.

Their interests are specific enough now to be a real conversation, and an interest survey is a fair launch point. Protect the consecutiveness — the ten days matter more than the two minutes, because what registers is that you came back.

Low-key and private. An adolescent will read a visible daily check-in as being singled out, so use hallway contact, the walk to class, or a written back-and-forth instead. Expect several days of nothing before anything opens; that stretch is the intervention, not a sign it's failing.

Other situations

  • Split across two staff who share the student.
  • Advisory or homeroom slot — the built-in version.
  • Written back-and-forth (note, journal) for students who prefer text to talk.
  • Team version — several teachers each take a different student.
  • Extend past ten days when momentum is building.

Evidence

Wlodkowski's "two-minute intervention" is the origin, later popularized in Allen Mendler's Connecting with Students. The frequently quoted 85% behavior-improvement figure is practitioner-reported and illustrative — treat it as a story about what became possible, not a measured finding. Repeating it as a research result overstates what exists.

What holds independently of that figure is the mechanism, which both lenses support: belonging conditions motivation and students engage more where they feel accepted (Deans for Impact, SoL 2nd ed. 2026, Q4); feedback and correction are received through the relationship rather than despite it (SoL Q2); and relational deposit made before the hard moment is what allows a later redirect to land without spending the bond (Klevan 2021).

Translation notes

Teachers often interpret 2×10 as a compliance lever — "spend two minutes, get better behavior." The underlying design principle is broader: relationship built before the hard moment is what lets later redirection land without spending the bond.

The non-academic framing is not incidental. It is the part that keeps the contact from reading as correction. A conversation about school or behavior is not 2×10 — it is another interaction in the correction ledger.

The tell that it's working isn't better behavior. It's that you know something about this student that has nothing to do with their grade.
