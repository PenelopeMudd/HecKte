5.1
Behavior & Engagement
At K–2, behavior and engagement are about structure and relationship — not skill deficits. Students are learning what school is. Procedures practiced in September are the engagement strategy for June.
Students have learned school’s rhythms but are developing academic identity. Engagement dips when the work feels pointless or the social stakes feel high. Choice, relevance, and effort-focused feedback are the main levers.
Adolescents are calibrating authenticity — watching whether the adult means what they say. Behavior problems in middle school are almost always belonging or autonomy problems in disguise.

5.2
Time, Space & Flow
Every transition is a procedure to be explicitly taught, not managed. The younger the students, the more scaffolded the signal — visual schedules, physical cues, practiced sequences.
Students can manage more of the transition themselves if the routine has been explicitly taught. Student roles in procedures reduce teacher load and build ownership.
Efficiency is the frame adolescents respond to. Bell work that starts the moment they enter removes the transition cost almost entirely.
Transitions both

Movement between activities — when taught as a procedure, reduces extraneous cognitive load to near zero.

5.3
Culture & Belonging
Belonging at K–2 is physical and immediate — am I seen? do I have a role? Greeting by name, classroom jobs, and consistent routines are the belonging infrastructure. Culture is built before November or not at all.
Peer relationships become primary at 3–5. Belonging is about having a place in the peer group, not just with the teacher. Class meetings and collaborative structures that make student contributions visible are the culture-building work.
Identity is the frame at 6–8. Adolescents need to know their presence matters and that the teacher sees them as a person, not a behavior profile. 2x10 is the highest-leverage single practice at this band.

Science of Learning — supporting nodes

Cognitive Load

The mind’s workspace is limited; extraneous load (from confusion, unpredictable routines, unclear instructions) crowds out the germane load that is the actual learning. Linked from: transitions, routines, expectations & norms, fair process.

Working Memory

The small, active store where thinking happens. Automaticity from rehearsed routines frees it for complex work. Linked from: routines, visual supports, expectations & norms.

Attention

Finite and easily captured by whatever is most salient — not always the lesson. Protected by clean transitions and low load; sustaining it is what engagement depends on. Linked from: transitions, engagement.
